Basketclock Remote Wiring

The 15 buttons form a key matrix of 3 rows and 5 columns. Each button has two legs: one goes to its row wire, the other to its column wire. The ESP32 drives one row low at a time and reads which column goes low.

1 · Panel, seen from the wiring side

Each button shows its two legs: the left dot is its row wire and the right dot its column wire. Buttons with the same dot colour are chained with one wire.

2 · Key matrix to the ESP32

The same wiring as a schematic. Rows run across, columns run down, and each box is a button between the two.

3 · Status LED

An RGB LED with a common cathode, wired through the small green perfboard. The perfboard's one resistor sits in the red line; green, blue and ground pass straight through. On the LED, the leg order is red, ground (longest leg), green, blue (shortest leg).

Resistor per colour: R = (3.3 V − Vf) / I, with the ESP32 pin at 3.3 V and 5–10 mA through the LED (20 mA is the pin's comfortable maximum). Use the forward voltages from the LED's datasheet if it has one.

Green and blue have only 0.1–0.3 V left over at 3.3 V, so a larger resistor such as 220 Ω makes them very dim (about 1 mA). Balance the colours with the resistor values, not in the code: a lower value gives a brighter colour. The firmware already caps the brightness at about 20% PWM.
